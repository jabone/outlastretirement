Test 1

Turn off the internet

  1. Open the app once so it saves itself to your browser. A note saying Ready to work offline appears when it’s done.
  2. Turn on airplane mode or disconnect Wi‑Fi.
  3. Reload the app and change any number — your annual spending, for example.
  4. Watch the plan recalculate. The verdict, the balances and the charts all update.

Why it works

There’s no server doing the math. The calculations run in your browser, on your device, and your plan is saved there too.

To be honest about it: the first visit needs a connection to download the app, and the optional Live Pricing refresh needs one too. This page and the home page aren’t saved for offline use; the app is.

This test works on a phone as well as a computer.

Test 2

Watch the network

  1. Open developer tools. Chrome, Edge or Firefox: press F12, or Cmd+Option+I on a Mac. Safari: first turn on Settings → Advanced → Show features for web developers, then press Cmd+Option+I.
  2. Click the Network tab.
  3. Reload the page.
  4. Build or edit a plan and keep an eye on the list.

What you’ll see

  • Only files from www.outlastretirement.com
  • After your first visit, most rows say (ServiceWorker): they came from the copy saved on your device, not the internet

What you won’t see

  • Uploads of your plan
  • Other companies’ domains
  • Ad or tracking scripts

Two things appear only if you turn them on. Live Pricing shows requests to Google (script.google.com) when you press Refresh, carrying ticker symbols at most. Anonymous analytics, if you opt in, shows requests to /_vercel/insights on our own domain. Browser extensions can add rows of their own; a private window without extensions gives the cleanest list.

Developer tools need a computer. On a phone, the airplane-mode test (Test 1) is the one to try.

Test 3

The browser enforces it

Every page we send comes with a rule sheet called a Content-Security-Policy. It tells your browser which addresses this site is allowed to contact.

Ours allows only our own site, plus Google’s spreadsheet servers for the optional Live Pricing. If any code tried to send data anywhere else, your browser would block it — not us, your browser.

See it yourself

  1. In developer tools, open the Network tab.
  2. Click the first request in the list (app/).
  3. Find Response Headers.
  4. Look for content-security-policy.

If the first request says (ServiceWorker), it came from the saved copy and shows no server headers. Open the Application tab, choose Service workers, tick Bypass for network and reload. Prefer not to open developer tools? Paste our address into a free header checker such as securityheaders.com.

Test 4

Read the code

The three tests above show what the app does. The source shows why it cannot do otherwise.

The app’s source is published at github.com/jabone/outlastretirement under the GNU Affero General Public License. What you can check there:

  • That the site runs the published code. Preferences shows the version the app is running (“Version 409”, say): the number of the newest entry in What’s New. The repository’s src/config/changelogLatest.js carries the same number, and a release is published there with every change to the site, so the two match. If the site ever shows a higher number than the repository, the repository is behind, and the Changelog page lists what the site has that the repository does not yet.
  • The policy the browser enforces (test 3) is vercel.json, and a test, src/__tests__/cspHeader.test.js, fails the build if anyone widens it.
  • Where a request could be made. The open app has no fetch of its own except the Premium module loader, src/premium/registry.js; the optional analytics (src/components/GatedAnalytics.jsx) and the update check (src/utils/pwa.js) are the other two things that touch the network, and all three ask src/utils/localOnlyMode.js first. Live Pricing’s URL rules are src/utils/pricingSource.js; its request is made by the Detailed Holdings module. src/__tests__/privacyTenets.test.js pins the privacy policy’s claims to the code.
  • What a Premium key carries. The loader sends it and nothing else; src/utils/premiumJwt.js is what reads it.

What is not there: the Premium modules themselves. They are a separate, closed work, fetched from our module server only when a key is present; what loads them, and the interface they are written against (PREMIUM_MODULE_CONTRACT.md), is in the open code. And the published source is the source of the site you are reading; we do not yet offer a way to prove byte-for-byte that the files your browser received were built from it, so treat the three tests above as the proof and the code as the explanation.

What we don’t claim

  • Loading the page does contact our host. That’s how the app gets to your browser in the first place. Our host, Vercel, receives your IP address and browser type, as any web server does; we don’t receive or keep them.
  • Google sees Live Pricing requests when you use it, as any website sees a visit: your IP address and the ticker symbols, nothing else. The Sheet’s script then looks the symbol up with Google Finance and Yahoo Finance from Google’s servers; those services see the symbol, not you.
  • We can’t see what’s on your device — so we can’t recover a lost plan. Keep backups.
